    01

    Build your world

    Drag equipment and terrain onto the Mars map. Click an inventory card then click the map to place it, or press Enter on the focused map to place it in the centre. One rover is allowed. Include the required obstacle minimum for your mission and spread the obstacles through your exploration area.

    02

    Plan your traverse

    Choose Route and click to add waypoints in order. Click close to a sample or station to snap a stop to it. Drag a numbered waypoint to move it; use its × button below the map to remove it.

    03

    Test and improve

    Preview your route, then write Python-style commands in the editor. Use roll() to move, collect() at samples and the cache, observe() at weather stations, and flash_colour_code("orange-blue") at a relay. Only a successful code run completes the mission. Read flight support and revise your program.

    ⌨

    Design your way

    Select objects from the map or inspector list. Use arrow keys on the map to move a selected object (Shift for larger steps), or enter X and Y. Delete removes it. Ctrl+Z undoes and Ctrl+Y redoes. Escape cancels placement.

      Your Python-style coding reference

      Small commands. Big exploration.

      Movement uses the same distance model as Sphero Mission App 2: speed × 1.28 × seconds. Headings: 0° north, 90° east, 180° south, 270° west. Run code from your rover’s placed starting position.

      Move and turn

      speed = 70
roll(speed, 90, 2)
turn(90)
roll(50)
set_heading(0)

      roll(speed, heading, seconds): speed 0–100, heading 0–359, seconds 0–10. Heading defaults to your current direction; seconds defaults to 1. turn() is relative; set_heading() is absolute.

      Perform science actions

      collect()
observe()
flash_colour_code("orange-blue")

      Move within 55 map units first. collect() collects one nearby sample, records weather, or deposits carried samples at a cache. observe() reads a weather station. The relay requires orange-blue; transmit() is a shorthand.

      Landing and Earth return

      land()
launch()
stop()

      These are separate mission actions, not a complete program. In Rank 1, reach your landing zone before land(), then signal at a relay. In Rank 4, collect and cache two samples, signal at a relay, reach your Earth-return capsule and call launch(). The return flight is simulated; the rover remains on Mars.

      Repeat with a loop

      for step in range(3):
  roll(40, 90, 0.5)
  say(step)

      This runs three times: step is 0, 1, then 2. The end of range() is excluded. Indent the commands inside a block with two spaces. Choose a safe distance for your own terrain.

      Use variables and decisions

      count = 0
while count < 2:
  if count == 0:
    set_color("blue")
  else:
    set_color("orange")
  count = count + 1

      Numbers, text, arithmetic, comparisons, and / or / not conditions are supported. Loops have a limit. Always update a while-loop variable so the loop finishes.

      Messages and pauses

      say("Ready for science")
set_color("orange")
wait(1)
stop()

      say() writes to flight support. wait() pauses up to 10 seconds. stop() ends the program and checks your science goals. Colours: blue, orange, white, green, red, purple, teal and yellow.

      Prove your mission works

      Route preview is a planning tool. Driving past a site during a code run does not complete its science action. Write the action explicitly. Your program must avoid hazards, stay inside the map, meet the resource limits, and complete your chosen mission’s goals.

      Python syntax and runtime errors show the relevant line number. Use the route calculations as a reference; write and test your own program.
